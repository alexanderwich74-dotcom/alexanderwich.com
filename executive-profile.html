Profile

Practical leadership in complex operational environments.

My career has taught me that strong operations depend on more than technical knowledge. They depend on clear priorities, capable people, reliable systems and the willingness to take responsibility when decisions need to be made.

Understanding the whole operation.

My professional experience spans hotel operations and IT, Facility and Technical Operations, as well as responsibility for assets, budgets and projects.

For me, the key is not to look at individual areas in isolation. A hotel operates reliably when operations, technology, business and people work together.

Thinking across departments.

My strength often lies at the interfaces between different parts of an organisation. Good solutions emerge when requirements are considered together and the impact on the wider operation is understood.

We can only win together.

Understanding technology, using specialists well.

My technical knowledge has developed over many years through operational responsibility, commissioning, system handovers and supplier-led training.

I do not replace specialist engineers, technicians or qualified suppliers. My role is to understand systems well enough to recognise dependencies, ask the right questions, coordinate specialists and assess decisions in the context of the wider operation.

Thinking commercially.

Technical and operational decisions always have a commercial dimension. CAPEX and OPEX, lifecycle costs, energy use, downtime risk, supplier performance and investment priorities all form part of the decision-making picture.

The goal is not the most technically elaborate solution, but the solution that makes long-term sense for the operation, the people and the asset.

Responsibility before hierarchy.

Responsibility means being present, making decisions, setting priorities and providing direction when pressure is high.

Leadership does not mean having every answer personally. It means ensuring that the right people, information and decisions come together.

Building structures that last.

Sustainable improvements come from clear responsibilities, robust processes, useful documentation and knowledge that does not depend on one individual.

That is why maintenance structures, system documentation, supplier governance, technical handovers and transparent decision paths matter just as much as solving the immediate problem.
